The average house price in the Yorkshire and The Humber region of England is £207,945 as of June 2026. This is what they have cost since January 1995, in today's money rather than in the cash prices of the day.

Over the last 10 years the average home in Yorkshire and The Humber has gained 3.7% in real terms, on a cash price 46.9% higher. Adjusted for inflation, home prices in Yorkshire and The Humber peaked in July 2007 and are 15.6% below that today.

The average detached price in the Yorkshire and The Humber region of England is £346,519 as of June 2026. This is what they have cost since January 1995, in today's money rather than in the cash prices of the day.

Over the last 10 years the average detached home in Yorkshire and The Humber has gained 1.6% in real terms, on a cash price 43.9% higher. Adjusted for inflation, detached home prices in Yorkshire and The Humber peaked in July 2007 and are 14.3% below that today.

The average semi-detached price in the Yorkshire and The Humber region of England is £214,613 as of June 2026. This is what they have cost since January 1995, in today's money rather than in the cash prices of the day.

Over the last 10 years the average semi-detached home in Yorkshire and The Humber has gained 6.8% in real terms, on a cash price 51.3% higher. Adjusted for inflation, semi-detached home prices in Yorkshire and The Humber peaked in July 2007 and are 12.1% below that today.

The average terraced price in the Yorkshire and The Humber region of England is £168,541 as of June 2026. This is what they have cost since January 1995, in today's money rather than in the cash prices of the day.

Over the last 10 years the average terraced home in Yorkshire and The Humber has gained 5.6% in real terms, on a cash price 49.6% higher. Adjusted for inflation, terraced home prices in Yorkshire and The Humber peaked in July 2007 and are 14.7% below that today.

The average flat price in the Yorkshire and The Humber region of England is £127,599 as of June 2026. This is what they have cost since January 1995, in today's money rather than in the cash prices of the day.

Over the last 10 years the average flat home in Yorkshire and The Humber has lost 8.9% of its value in real terms, even though the cash price is 29.1% higher. Adjusted for inflation, flat home prices in Yorkshire and The Humber peaked in May 2007 and are 31.7% below that today.
